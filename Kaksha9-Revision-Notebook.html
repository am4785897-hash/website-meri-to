Science — Exploration

Entering the World of Secondary Science
CLASS 9

1.Why Secondary-Stage Science Feels Different

  • In Classes 6–8, science was mostly about observing and describing things around you.
  • From Class 9 onwards, science asks you to go deeper — not just "what happens" but "how do we know it happens, and why should we trust it?"
  • This chapter is a bridge: it teaches the thinking tools you'll use in every chapter this year.

2.The Scientific Method — Step by Step

Observation → Question → Hypothesis → Experiment (Evidence) → Analysis → Conclusion → Refinement/Repeat
  • Observation: Noticing something in nature (e.g. "ice melts faster in sunlight").
  • Question: Turning the observation into a question.
  • Hypothesis: A testable explanation — not a random guess.
  • Experiment: Controlled testing where only one variable changes — a fair test.
  • Conclusion: Based on evidence, not opinion.
  • Refinement: Science is never "final" — new evidence can update old conclusions.

3.Scientific Models

A model is a simplified version of something real, built to help us understand or predict it.

  • Physical models — e.g. a ball-and-stick model of an atom.
  • Diagrammatic models — e.g. the water cycle diagram.
  • Mathematical models — e.g. an equation predicting how far an object falls.
RememberModels are useful but incomplete — they simplify reality for a purpose.

4.The Language of Science

  • Science uses symbols, units and equations to communicate precisely worldwide.
  • SI Units are the global standard: metre, kilogram, second, kelvin.
  • Precision matters — "the object is fast" isn't scientific; "the object moves at 12 m/s" is.

5.Laws, Theories & Principles

TermWhat it doesCan it change?Example
LawDescribes a consistent patternRarelyNewton's Laws of Motion
TheoryExplains why, backed by strong evidenceCan be refinedTheory of Evolution
PrincipleGeneral rule guiding reasoningGenerally stableArchimedes' Principle
Key insightA "theory" in science is NOT a guess — it's one of the strongest forms of scientific knowledge.
⚠ Common Mistakes
  • Thinking "theory" means a weak/unproven idea.
  • Believing models are 100% real, not simplified.
  • Skipping units in numerical answers.
  • Science = observation + testable questions + evidence-based conclusions.
  • Models simplify reality to aid understanding — useful, not perfect.
  • Laws describe, theories explain, principles guide.
  • SI units + symbols allow universal scientific communication.
  • This year integrates Physics, Chemistry, Biology & Earth Science as one subject.

1.Introduction — Cell: The Building Block of Life

Ye chapter batata hai ki har living organism chhote-chhote units se bana hota hai jinhe cell kehte hain. Cell life ki basic unit hai — chahe woh ek single-celled bacteria ho ya multi-cellular human body.

2.Key Concepts

  • Cell Theory: Sab living organisms cells se bane hote hain, aur cell hi structural & functional unit of life hai.
  • Cell ki khoj: Robert Hooke (1665) ne ki thi (cork ke slice mein "cells" dekhe).
  • Cell Wall: Sirf plant cells, fungi aur bacteria mein — rigid protective outer layer.
  • Cell Membrane: Sabhi cells mein — selectively permeable.
  • Nucleus: Cell ka control center — DNA/genetic material yahan hota hai.
  • Cytoplasm: Jelly-like substance jisme organelles float karte hain.

3.Important Definitions

  • Mitochondria = powerhouse; Nucleus = control center; Ribosomes = protein factory.
  • Plant cells mein cell wall + chloroplast hota hai, animal cells mein nahi.
  • Osmosis aur diffusion cell ke movement processes hain jo exam mein important hain.

Social Science

Understanding Society: India and Beyond
CLASS 9

1.What is Social Science, Really?

  • The systematic, evidence-based study of human society — how people live, organise, govern, and use resources.
  • NOT just memorising facts — it's about asking questions, finding evidence, and understanding patterns.
  • Helps us become informed citizens who make better decisions.

2.What Makes it "Scientific"?

Question → Collect Information → Analyse → Interpret → Draw Conclusion → Reflect & Ask New Questions

Evidence can come from documents, maps, statistics, interviews, observations, photographs, and artefacts.

3.Branches of Social Science

BranchWhat it studiesExamples
HistoryThe past — events, people, changesIndependence of India, French Revolution
GeographyEarth's surface, people-environmentClimate, natural resources, population
Political SciencePower, government, democracy, lawsIndian Constitution, elections
EconomicsHow people produce, distribute & use resourcesMarkets, money, poverty
SociologySociety, social groups, institutions, cultureFamily, caste, gender roles

4.Guiding Values

  • Diversity — respecting different cultures, regions, viewpoints.
  • Inclusivity — fair representation of all groups.
  • Sustainability — long-term impact on environment & resources.
  • Equity — fairness in opportunity and treatment.
Why Integrated Approach?Real issues rarely fit one box. A drought (Geography) affects farmers' income (Economics), which can influence local elections (Political Science).
  • Social Science = 5 connected disciplines studied through evidence.
  • Core guiding values: Diversity, Inclusivity, Sustainability, Equity.
  • Real-world issues usually involve multiple disciplines together.
  • Good social scientists are curious, patient, open-minded and respectful.
  • This is the foundation chapter — sets up how to think for the rest of the year.

1.Introduction — Shaping of the Earth's Surface

Ye chapter Earth ke andar ki structure, plate tectonics, aur Earth ki surface kaise banti hai — ye sab cover karta hai.

2.Key Concepts

  • Earth's Interior Layers: Crust → Mantle → Outer Core → Inner Core
  • Plate Tectonics Theory: Earth ki crust bahut saare "plates" mein divided hai jo dheere-dheere move karti hain.
  • Types of Plate Boundaries:
    • Convergent — plates aapas mein takrati hain (mountains, e.g. Himalayas)
    • Divergent — plates alag hoti hain (new crust, e.g. mid-ocean ridges)
    • Transform — plates side-by-side slide (earthquakes, e.g. San Andreas Fault)

3.Important Definitions

TermMeaning
CrustEarth ki sabse upar wali thin layer
MantleCrust ke neeche wala thick, semi-solid layer
CoreEarth ka sabse andar wala hissa (mostly iron-nickel)
Tectonic PlateBade rigid rock ke tukde jo Earth's crust banate hain
Continental DriftTheory ki continents kabhi ek saath the aur dheere-dheere alag hue

4.Earth's Layers

LayerThickness / NatureState
Crust~5–70 kmSolid
Mantle~2900 kmSemi-solid
Outer Core~2200 kmLiquid
Inner Core~1250 km (radius)Solid

5.Flowchart: Plate Movement → Landform

Tectonic plates move (slowly, few cm/year) ┌──────────┼──────────┐ Convergent Divergent Transform Mountains New crust/ Earthquakes (Himalayas) Ocean ridges (no new land)
  • Earth ke 4 main layers: Crust, Mantle, Outer Core, Inner Core.
  • Tectonic plates continuously move — bahut slow, lekin continuously.
  • 3 tarah ki plate boundaries: Convergent, Divergent, Transform.
  • Himalayas convergent boundary ka example hai.
  • Plate tectonics earthquakes, volcanoes aur mountain-building explain karti hai.

  • Builds the skill of describing position precisely.

2.The Cartesian Coordinate System

  • X-axis → horizontal line
  • Y-axis → vertical line
  • Origin (0,0) → where both axes meet
  • A point is written as an ordered pair (x, y)

3.The Four Quadrants

Quadrantx-signy-signExample
I++(3, 4)

4.Distance & Midpoint Formulas

Distance AB = √[(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²] Midpoint M = ( (x₁ + x₂)/2 , (y₁ + y₂)/2 )

Distance formula derived from Pythagoras Theorem. Midpoint is simply the average of coordinates.

Worked Example: A(2,3), B(5,7) Distance = √[(5−2)² + (7−3)²] = √[9+16] = √25 = 5 units
Midpoint = ( (2+5)/2, (3+7)/2 ) = (3.5, 5)
  • A point is located as (x, y) using two perpendicular axes.
  • Four quadrants have fixed +/− sign patterns.
  • Distance formula comes from Pythagoras' theorem.
  • Midpoint = average of the x's, average of the y's.
  • Useful for graphs, geometry, GPS, and maps.

1.Introduction — Linear Polynomials

Ye chapter algebra ki foundation hai — variables, coefficients, aur especially linear polynomials (degree 1) ko real-life examples se samjhaya gaya hai.

2.Key Concepts

  • Polynomial: Expression jisme variables aur constants ho, sirf +, −, × operations aur non-negative whole number powers ho.
  • Degree of Polynomial: Variable ka highest power.
  • Linear Polynomial: Degree 1 — form: y = ax + b
  • Slope (a) batata hai line kitni "steep" hai; y-intercept (b) batata hai line y-axis ko kahan cross karti hai.

3.Important Definitions

TermMeaning
VariableLetter jo unknown value represent karta hai
CoefficientNumber jo variable ke saath multiply hota hai
Constant TermFixed number, jisme variable nahi hota
Linear GrowthJab value constant amount se badhti hai
Linear DecayJab value constant amount se ghatit hai

4.Formulas

General form: p(x) = ax + b (a ≠ 0) Slope-Intercept: y = mx + c Zero of polynomial: ax + b = 0 ⇒ x = −b/a

5.Flowchart: Analyze a Linear Polynomial

Real-life situation (e.g., auto fare) ↓ Identify variable & constant ↓ Write as p(x) = ax + b ↓ Find degree, coefficient, constant term ↓ Plot on graph → straight line
  • Linear polynomial ka degree hamesha 1 hota hai — form ax + b.
  • Coefficient variable ke saath hota hai, constant term alag khada rehta hai.
  • Har linear polynomial ko graph pe straight line ki tarah represent kar sakte hain.
  • Zero nikalne ke liye equation ko 0 ke barabar rakho.
  • Real-life mein linear patterns: fare, tank filling, savings growth.

1.परिचय — "क्या लिखूँ?"

यह एक आत्मपरक (personal) निबंध है जिसमें लेखक अपने अनुभव से बताते हैं कि निबंध लिखना कितना कठिन काम है — खासकर जब विषय बहुत बड़ा हो।

2.मुख्य बिंदु

  • लेखक को दो विषयों पर निबंध लिखना था: "दूर के ढोल सुहावने होते हैं" और "समाज-सुधार"।
  • लेखक के पास गार्डिनर जैसी स्वतःस्फूर्त मानसिक स्थिति नहीं थी।
  • उनके पास न विश्वकोश था, न कोई reference book।
  • निबंधशास्त्र के आचार्यों के परस्पर विरोधी विचार — किसी ने छोटे निबंध की सलाह दी, किसी ने सरल भाषा की।

3.महत्वपूर्ण परिभाषाएँ

शब्दअर्थ
आत्मपरक निबंधलेखक के अपने विचारों और अनुभवों पर आधारित निबंध।
निबंध-शास्त्रनिबंध लिखने के नियम और सिद्धांत।

  • पहचान: आलोचक, कवि, निबंधकार और हास्य-व्यंग्यकार।

5.Flowchart: निबंध लिखने की समस्या

  • यह निबंध बताता है कि लेखन ज्ञान से ज़्यादा विचार और कल्पना की कला है।
  • लेखक को दो कठिन विषयों पर निबंध लिखना पड़ा था।
  • निबंध-शास्त्र के आचार्यों के विरोधी विचार लेखक को उलझाते हैं।

1.About the Poem — भारतीवसन्तगीतिः

  • A गीति (song/lyrical poem) addressed to वाणी (Vani / सरस्वती) — goddess of speech, knowledge and music.
  • The poet requests the goddess to play her वीणा and sing a fresh song welcoming वसन्त ऋतु (spring).
  • Vasanta symbolises renewal, beauty, and creative inspiration.

2.Structure & Content

  • आम्रवृक्षाः शोभन्ते — mango trees look beautiful (blossoming).
  • Fresh flowers blooming, nature turning vibrant.
  • External renewal of nature connects with internal renewal of creativity.
Central ideaSpring brings beauty outside, and the goddess' song brings inspiration inside.

1.परिचय — "सुखस्य मूलं धर्मः, धर्मस्य मूलम् अर्थः"

यह अध्याय एक महत्वपूर्ण जीवन-सिद्धान्त को समझाता है — सुख का मूल धर्म है, और धर्म का मूल अर्थ है, जिसका सही तरीके से अर्जन एवं उपयोग किया जाए।

2.मुख्य बिंदु

  • सुखस्य मूलं धर्मः — Happiness की जड़ धर्म (righteous conduct) में है।
  • धर्मस्य मूलम् अर्थः — धर्म की जड़ अर्थ (wealth) में है — धन का सही अर्जन और उपयोग धर्म का भाग है।
  • यह अध्याय नैतिक मूल्यों, अनुशासन और संतुलित जीवन पर बल देता है।
  • धन का बुद्धिमानी से उपयोग करना धर्ममय जीवन के लिए आवश्यक है।

3.महत्वपूर्ण संस्कृत शब्द

4.अन्य महत्वपूर्ण विचार

  • अर्थ का अर्जन परिश्रम, ईमानदारी और उचित मार्ग से होना चाहिए।
  • धर्म का पालन करने से व्यक्ति, समाज और राष्ट्र — सभी का कल्याण होता है।
  • सुख स्थायी तभी होता है जब वह धर्म पर आधारित हो।

5.Concept Chain

धर्मः (Righteous conduct) ↓ (leads to) सुखम् (Happiness) अर्थः (Wealth, सही तरीके से earned) ↓ (foundation of) धर्मः (Righteous conduct)
  • अध्याय का मुख्य संदेश — सुख की जड़ धर्म में है।
  • धर्म के सही पालन के लिए अर्थ का सही उपयोग जरूरी है।
  • यह अध्याय नैतिक और ethical जीवन को महत्व देता है।
  • धन कमाना बुरा नहीं, लेकिन सही तरीके से कमाना और उपयोग करना महत्वपूर्ण है।
  • यह एक मूल्य-आधारित पाठ है जो संतुलित और अनुशासित जीवन सिखाता है।
